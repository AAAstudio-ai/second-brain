The second brain, or “vault”

—The same AI, in two companies, is not the same tool at all.

Almost nobody works this way yet.
It will probably be ordinary in a few years,
so those who start now are ahead of the curve.

Every card below opens. Click or tap a title.

A second brain, also called a vault, is a folder of ordinary text files on your own computer that holds everything an AI needs to know about your business, so that it stops starting from zero every morning.

This page explains what a vault is, how it gets built and what it changes, without describing any particular company or any particular AI.

How it worksYou talk, it writes. You never touch a file.

1It is a folder. That is all.Nothing to buy. The folder itself costs nothing.
  • Ordinary text files, sitting on your computer or on a server you control.
  • You can open any of them and read it yourself, without a special tool.
  • It is not a product. It is a folder, like the ones you already have.
  • It adds no subscription of its own. You already pay for an AI; the vault is what you plug it into.
2You never write any of itYou talk. The AI writes.
  • You speak to the AI in plain language, the way you would brief a colleague.
  • There is no special phrasing to learn: the quality of the answer comes from what it already knows about your company, not from how you ask.
  • It writes the file, gives it a name, and puts it where it belongs.
  • You can work with a vault every day for months and never create or edit a single file by hand.

For example

You say

“We decided this morning: nothing goes out to a client before I have seen it. Remember that.”

It writes, on its own

A short note in the memory: the rule, the date, and who decided it. And because it is a rule, it also goes into the limits.

From then on, whenever it prepares something meant for a client, it stops and asks you first. Six months later, it still does. You did not open a file, choose a name, or decide where it goes.

That is the whole trick: the folder is yours, but you are not the one tidying it.
3What you never have to doNo tool to learn, nothing to file.
  • You never have to learn new software: you write in a conversation window, in your own words.
  • You never have to organise anything: it decides where a thing belongs, and puts it there.
  • You never have to keep it up to date: at the end of an exchange, it writes down what changed.
4And you can still lookNothing is hidden from you.
  • Open the folder and everything is there in plain sight, in readable sentences.
  • You can correct a line by hand if you want to. Most people never need to.
  • Nothing is locked in a format only a machine can read: any file opens and reads like a normal document.
  • Who is allowed to open what stays your decision. Some parts are shared with the team, others stay private, and anything too sensitive is simply kept out of the folder.
Usually the moment it clicks: it is neither magic nor fragile, and it belongs to you.

What is insideSeven parts, and what each one does.

1Your documents, and a mapWhat you already have, tidied.
  • Your real working documents, sorted by subject: one folder per client, one per project.
  • One file, called the map, says what is kept where.
  • So nothing is ever searched for at random, by the AI or by your team.
2The instructionsWhat it reads before it says anything.

Think of the note you would hand a new assistant on their first morning: who we are, what we sell, how we speak to clients, and what we never do.

  • Here it is one file, and it is read at the start of every conversation, not only the first one.
  • You write it once, and you change one line when a rule changes.
What it is for: it works your way from the first sentence, six months later included.
3The memoryWhat was decided, and why.

Each time something is decided, learned or corrected, a short note is written: what, when, and the reason behind it.

  • Before answering, the AI reads those notes again.
  • They are written as plain sentences, not as machine data, so they are still understandable months later.
What it is for: this is the difference between an AI that repeats a mistake and one that stops making it.
4The methodsThe way things are done here.

How a quote is prepared in your company. How a client visit is prepared. How the monthly report comes out.

  • Written down once, the way you would explain it to someone joining the team.
  • When the situation comes up, it applies the method instead of asking you to describe it again. And you can always point it at the right one.
What it is for: anything your team repeats can become one. This is where the hours are saved.
5The limitsWhat it is never allowed to do.

A vault does not only say what to do. It says what must never happen, and those lines are read before every answer.

  • Some are plain sentences you write yourself: never contact a client directly, never touch these files, never send anything before I have seen it.
  • Others are enforced by the system: an action such as deleting a file or sending a message is stopped and handed back to you for approval.
  • You start with approvals almost everywhere, then remove them one by one, only where trust has been earned.
Why it holds: a limit written into the system does not depend on the AI remembering it. It is not a promise of good behaviour, it is a door that will not open.
6The reflexesThings that happen without anyone asking.

Bringing back a postponed decision on the day it is due. Checking a document before it leaves for a client. Refusing to delete a file. Nobody has to remember them.

  • Written once, and they hold even when nobody is thinking about them.
  • What is possible here depends on the AI in place, so this part is shaped around what you already use.
7The connectorsOptional, and always last.

A connector is what lets the AI open one of your tools by itself, instead of you copying and pasting from one window into another.

Mailboxes, shared calendars, file storage, project tracking: most of the software a company runs on can be reached this way, and a growing number of them now provide a connector ready to use, built and maintained either by the software company or by the AI provider.

Without one. You paste a supplier's last three emails into the conversation.
With one. You ask, and it goes and reads them.

  • You grant it the way you click “sign in with Google” on a new website: access to one thing, with a defined scope, and to nothing else.
  • Where a tool provides none, one can usually be set up for it. Finding out which of yours already have one is quick, and costs nothing.
  • And a vault works perfectly well without a single connector. That is where everyone starts.

Why it changes thingsWhere almost everyone is today, and what happens after.

1Today: you ask, then you rewriteWhere almost everyone is.
  • It has read almost everything, and it knows nothing about you: not your clients, not your prices, not how you work.
  • So it is very good on a general question, and vague as soon as the answer depends on your company.
What it costs: you do the last part by hand, every single time.
2Then you learn to write better promptsUseful, and no longer where the difference is.
  • You give a role, a tone, a few examples. The answers really do improve, and for a while this was the whole skill.
  • But you are still the one supplying, by hand, the context it does not have.
  • And what you explain in one conversation serves that conversation only.
The shift almost nobody noticed: how you ask matters far less than what it already knows about you when you ask. That is where the quality comes from now.
3You explain, then it is erasedThe part nobody talks about.
  • You describe the company, the project, the constraints, and the conversation finally becomes useful.
  • You close the window. Nothing is kept. Tomorrow you start again.
What it costs: the same briefing dozens of times a year. It is like working with someone brilliant who forgets your company every single evening.
4The turn: it already knows the houseThis is where a vault begins.

Everything above happens without a vault. From here on, it exists.

  • The context now lives in your folder, so every conversation reads it and you never explain it again.
  • It is not frozen: it gets corrected as the company changes.
What changes: the first answer is already right, instead of the fourth one.
5And then it compoundsThe part that is hard to catch up with.
  • At the end of an exchange, what was decided or corrected is written down, and read back the next time.
  • So a correction made once holds, and you never say the same thing twice.
What changes: after six months, someone starting from zero every morning simply cannot catch up. The gap does not stay the same, it widens every week.
6And it stays yoursThe step almost nobody knows about.
  • The instructions, the memory, the methods: all of it is ordinary text files, in your folder, on your machine.
  • The AI is only the engine plugged into them. Change the engine and the folder does not move.
  • If the price triples or the service closes, you plug in another one and carry on the next day.
What changes: you rent an engine. You do not rent your own memory.

A day without

You open four files to find where a project stands. In the evening, what was decided is written nowhere.

A day with

You ask, and you get the answer. In the evening, what was decided goes into the memory.

The fair questionsThe doubts, answered plainly.

?Does it matter which AI I use?

Less than people think, but not nothing.

  • The vault is the same folder whichever AI you plug into it, and so is everything it holds about your company.
  • What differs is the way of working with it. Some AI tools can open the vault, write in it and carry out a series of steps on their own. Others mainly read and answer, and you do more of the moves yourself.
In short: the destination is the same, the road is a little different. A vault is built around the AI you already have, not the other way round.
?Where does my data actually go?
  • The vault stays on your machine. It is never uploaded anywhere as a whole, and nobody hosts it for you.
  • To answer a question, the part needed for that question is sent to your AI provider, processed under the terms of your business plan, and not used to train the model.
  • Whatever must never leave the company stays outside the scope, and that is agreed in writing before anything starts.
Worth saying plainly: this is the same question you already answer for the AI tools in place today.
?“I already have a folder of notes.”
  • A folder of notes is a tidy drawer: somebody has to remember to open it, and nobody does.
  • A vault is read automatically before every answer, and written into afterwards.
The difference: it is not about the filing. It is that nobody has to remember.
?And if it does something stupid?
  • Deleting, sending, publishing, overwriting a file: each of these asks you first.
  • At the start you put approvals everywhere, then you remove them where trust has been earned.
  • The rule is written into the system. It does not depend on the AI behaving well.
?And if the person using it leaves?
  • Today, when someone leaves, their way of working leaves with them.
  • Here it is written down in the company's own folder, in plain sentences, and it stays when the person goes.
  • The next person starts from what is already there, instead of rebuilding it.

The difference, put simply. Without a vault you work with a brilliant colleague who remembers nothing. With one, you work with someone who knows the house.
